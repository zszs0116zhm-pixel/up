By Han Xiankai

A Field Guide to Beginning Again.

This is not a book about winning. It is one ordinary person's account of how to stop falling after a company collapses, a body breaks, and relationships strain — and then how to begin learning again through English, AI, real work, and ninety days that answer to evidence.

There is no curve that only rises. There is failure, insomnia, going home, doctors, and apologies; there are also pages read again, sentences written, and small things delivered. Progress, most of the time, means sweeping the floor, saying what needs to be said, and finishing the next step that can honestly be finished today.

What this book is: a bilingual manuscript centred on recovery, a testable learning method, a set of copyable worksheets, and a path that starts at five minutes, retests by chapter, and looks back after ninety days.

What it is not: a success formula, medical or therapeutic advice, or a course that requires you to check in.

Who This Book Is For ​

  • People who want to begin again and do not know which page to start from;
  • Self-directed learners who need English for real tasks, not only an exam guide;
  • People using AI for real work who do not want to hand over judgment, sources, and privacy with it;
  • People coming out of failure, interruption, or a long low season, who need to catch themselves first;
  • Parents and teachers who want to support a learner without imposing an adult's pace.

If you are looking for a quick formula, a complete English curriculum, or clinical treatment advice, this may not be the right entrance. The full boundaries are in Who This Book Is For — and Not For.

Bring One Page Back to Today ​

Lifelong learning is not opening ten courses at once. It is letting one page become a small reality today:

  1. Write down the problem in front of you. In Learning State, record what you already know, what is missing, and what counts as complete. If the problem concerns capacity, relationships, or recovery, use the Life Practice Toolkit.
  2. Complete one visible action. Ask AI to help break it down, but check the sources and make the choice yourself. Leave notes, code, a recording, an email, feedback, or another real output — not only a saved chat.
  3. Come back and check whether it actually carried anything. Retest on the chapter's cycle, then use the Weekly Review or the 90-Day Cycle Map to decide whether the next step is worth continuing.

If you find an error, a translation difference, or a reproducible result, tell us through the issue templates. If this guide helped you finish something real, you can also star the repository so more learners can find it.

For Those Still on the Way ​

For Younger Readers ​

You do not have to find a permanently correct direction in your twenties. Many directions appear only after a few small projects, several kinds of work, and a few results you had to carry. More reliable than choosing a perfect track is keeping the ability to learn, deliver, and turn.

If career anxiety keeps you collecting courses, certificates, and success stories, try a two-week project instead: solve one problem for someone near you, build one tool that runs, finish one article with sources, or improve one process for a real user. The project may fail, but it will tell you what you like, what you lack, whether you can deliver, and whether anyone wants to use it again.

For Those in a Low Season ​

Job loss, a failed venture, the end of a relationship, damaged health, and long confusion all take a real toll. A low season is not an exam you must win immediately. What you can do today may be sleeping one relatively complete night, eating a meal, going outside, or telling one trusted person, “I need help now.” These things matter on their own. They do not have to be packaged first as a growth report.

A pause is not giving up, asking for help is not weakness, and recalibrating is not going backwards. When strength returns, decide whether to add weight. The personal stories here are not medical or therapeutic advice. If low mood, insomnia, despair, or dangerous thoughts persist, contact someone you trust and seek qualified local medical or psychological support. Safety first; growth after.

My name is Han Xiankai. This project began in 2017 as the Ridiculous English Learning Guide and later entered learning, development, enterprise services, business experiments, and ordinary life.
